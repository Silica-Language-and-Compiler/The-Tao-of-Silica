# Style decisions

Decisions about the book's voice and conventions, agreed with the author.
The `ghostwriter` skill reads this before every session.

## Standing rules

- **Focus (author's instruction, 2026-11-13).** This is not a book about how to
  write Silica code. It is about the ideas behind the code and the
  opportunities the design opens up. Each principle chapter follows the shape:
  the idea → where it came from → what it costs → **what it opens up**. Code
  terms appear only where they carry an idea; no listings or tutorials.

- **Quotation rule (author's instruction, 2026-11-13).** The author is never
  quoted in chapter body text — not from interviews and not from the author's
  own documents. Body text paraphrases. Short quotes (a phrase or one
  sentence) may appear in footnotes with their source. Full statements are
  kept here in `interviews/`.
- Quoting other people (papers, standards bodies, the Erlang documentation) in
  the body is fine with attribution; epigraphs must not be the author's words.
- Agent-recorded material from `.claude-memory` is not treated as the author's
  words until confirmed (`% TODO-VERIFY` in the .tex files).

## Decided

- **Voice: third person (author decision, 2026-11-13).** A narrator describes
  Barney and the work. Combined with the quotation rule, the author's views
  appear as paraphrase in the body, with short quotes only in footnotes.

## Open decisions

- **Chapter order:** Why? → History → ten principles. Confirm.
- **Epigraphs:** one per chapter, from sources other than the author?
